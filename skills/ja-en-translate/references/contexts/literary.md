# Context: literary

Fiction, personal essays (随筆), poetry, song-like prose, film/anime dialogue scripts (for reading, not subtitles), memoir.

## Defaults

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Japanese style | Follow the source voice; mixing is allowed when the source mixes (narration だ, dialogue です) |
| Keigo | Characterization, not politeness: reproduce each speaker's level |
| English | Literary register matched to the source (plain, lyrical, terse) |
| Notes | `none` in the text. Cultural explanations go in the analysis, or as endnotes only if the user asks |
| Restructuring | Medium to high, in service of voice and rhythm |

## Core stance

Translate **voice, rhythm, and what is left unsaid**. Japanese literary prose relies on implication, ellipsis, and sensory detail; over-explaining in English destroys it. Conversely, English literary prose made into 翻訳調 Japanese loses its music.

## JA→EN

- **Do not fill every gap.** If the source withholds a subject or a feeling, keep it withheld where English allows (fragments, "There was…", passive, free indirect style).
- **Sentence-final particles and 語尾 carry character**: 〜わ, 〜ぜ, 〜じゃん, 〜でござる, 関西弁. Render through English word choice, rhythm, and dialect markers sparingly — never by spelling out particles. Record each character's voice decisions in the analysis.
- **Onomatopoeia/mimetics** (しとしと, ざわざわ, ぽつんと): fold into verbs and adjectives ("drizzled", "a murmur rippled through"), not "shito-shito".
- **Honorific names** (〜さん, 〜ちゃん, 〜先輩, お兄ちゃん): decide once — drop, keep, or replace by relationship words — and apply consistently. Keeping honorifics is acceptable for anime/manga audiences; dropping is standard for general literary publishing. Record the choice.
- **Seasonal and cultural references** (縁側, 蝉時雨, お盆): keep the image; add minimal in-text context only if the reader would be lost.

## EN→JA

- Choose narration base: だ/た past narration is standard; avoid alternating た/る unless the source shifts tense for effect.
- Pronouns: 彼/彼女 sparingly. Use names, or omit. "He said" chains → dialogue without tags or with varied verbs.
- Give characters distinct 一人称 and 語尾 (僕/俺/私/あたし) based on characterization; record choices.
- Idioms and wordplay: find a Japanese equivalent of the effect; if impossible, keep the meaning and mark the loss in the analysis.
- Poetry: rhythm (七五調 is optional, not required), line breaks preserved, no explanatory additions.

## Critique focus

- Does the voice match (each character distinct, narrator consistent)?
- Was any implication made explicit unnecessarily?
- Read aloud: is the rhythm natural?
