# Context: marketing

Ads, product pages, slogans, brand copy, SNS posts, event announcements, posters, landing pages.

## Defaults

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Japanese style | です・ます (friendly) or 体言止め for headlines |
| Keigo | `teinei`, warm |
| English | Energetic, concise, active voice |
| Notes | `none` |
| Restructuring | Highest — transcreation |

## Core stance

**Effect over wording.** The translation must make the target reader feel and do what the source wants (buy, sign up, come). Literal accuracy matters only for facts: prices, dates, specs, legal claims.

## Rules

- **Headlines and slogans**: produce 2–3 alternatives with a one-line rationale each (in the summary, not in `translation.md`). Put the recommended one in `translation.md`.
- **Facts are fixed**: numbers, prices (税込/tax included), dates, conditions, and any regulated claim (health, finance) are translated exactly; no exaggeration added.
- **Japanese copy features**: 体言止め, 「」 emphasis, emotional adjectives (ワクワク, こだわりの). In English, convert to concrete benefits; "こだわりの" ≠ "particular" → "carefully crafted", "obsessively made".
- **English copy features**: imperatives ("Discover", "Get"), second person "you". In Japanese, drop "you", turn imperatives into invitations: "Get yours today" → 今すぐチェック / ぜひお試しください.
- **Hashtags**: keep originals; add target-language hashtags only if asked.
- **Cultural fit**: seasonal hooks (春の新生活, Black Friday) may need adaptation; record any substitution.
- **Length**: SNS limits and design space — note if the translation is much longer than the source.

## Critique focus

- Would a native copywriter publish this?
- Facts unchanged? No new claims?
- Brand tone consistent across pieces?
