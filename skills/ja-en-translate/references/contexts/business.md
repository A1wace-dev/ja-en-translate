# Context: business

Business email, cover letters, proposals, meeting minutes, internal memos, job applications (ES), correspondence with professors or offices when the tone is transactional.

## Defaults

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Japanese style | です・ます |
| Keigo | `sonkei-kenjo` for external/superior recipients; `teinei` for internal peers |
| English | Professional, warm, direct; contractions only in internal informal mail |
| Notes | `none` (put remarks outside the translation, in the summary) |
| Restructuring | High. Conventions of the target genre override source wording |

## Core stance

Business text is defined by **function and relationship**, not words. Translate the act (request, apology, decline, thanks, follow-up) into how that act is normally performed in the target culture.

## JA→EN

**Formulaic phrases are not translated literally.**

| Japanese | English approach |
|---|---|
| お世話になっております | Omit, or "I hope this finds you well" / "Thank you for your continued support" (sparingly) |
| よろしくお願いいたします (closing) | Match function: "Thank you for your consideration" / "I look forward to hearing from you" / "Best regards" |
| ご確認のほどよろしくお願いいたします | "Could you please review…" / "Please let me know if…" |
| 恐れ入りますが | "I'm sorry to trouble you, but" / or simply "Could you…" |
| ご査収ください | "Please find attached…" |
| 取り急ぎご連絡まで | "Just a quick note to let you know…" |
| 検討いたします | Ambiguous — may be a soft no. Translate as "We will consider it" and flag in analysis if context suggests refusal |

- Japanese indirection (〜かと存じます, 〜いただけますと幸いです) → English polite but more direct requests. Do not reproduce hedging layers literally; English readers read them as uncertainty.
- Subject line: rewrite to English convention (specific, short): 【ご相談】〜の件 → "Question about ..."
- Names and titles: 田中部長 → Mr./Ms. Tanaka (Department Manager) or just "Mr. Tanaka" in body; never "Tanaka Bucho".

## EN→JA

- Add the expected frame: 宛名 (〇〇株式会社 〇〇部 〇〇様), opening (お世話になっております / 突然のご連絡失礼いたします for first contact), closing (何卒よろしくお願いいたします).
- English directness → softened requests: "Please send X by Friday" → 金曜日までにXをお送りいただけますでしょうか.
- "I" largely disappears; use 私 only for contrast or self-introduction.
- First names in English mail → surname + 様 in Japanese unless the relationship is clearly casual.
- Avoid double keigo (二重敬語): おっしゃられる ✗ → おっしゃる; 拝見させていただく ✗ → 拝見する.
- Check さ入れ言葉 (読まさせていただく ✗).

## Professors and university offices

Messages to 先生 or 事務 are `business` with `sonkei-kenjo` (EN→JA), but the content may be academic. Use business formulas for the frame and academic terminology in the body.

## Critique focus

- Does each sentence perform the same act (request, refusal, apology) at the same strength?
- Is the keigo level consistent and free of 二重敬語?
- Would a native professional send this as is?

## Example

EN: Hi Professor Sato, could I get a one-week extension on the report? I've been sick.
JA: 佐藤先生
お世話になっております。〇〇学部の〇〇です。
体調を崩してしまい、レポートの提出期限を一週間ほど延長していただくことは可能でしょうか。
ご多忙のところ恐縮ですが、ご検討いただけますと幸いです。
何卒よろしくお願いいたします。
(Name and faculty are placeholders — mark them for the user to fill.)
