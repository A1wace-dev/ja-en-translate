# EN→JA Pitfalls

Checklist for analysis, drafting, and critique. The most common failure is 翻訳調 (translationese): grammatical Japanese that no native writer would produce.

## 1. Pronoun overuse

- Delete "I / you / he / she / it / they" whenever context makes them clear.
- あなた is almost never right in prose or instructions; use the person's name, a role (お客様, 皆さん), or nothing.
- 彼/彼女 chains → name, role, or omission. 「それは」 for "it" → usually delete or use この/その + noun.

## 2. Inanimate subjects (無生物主語)

"The data shows…" / "This decision led him to…" → re-center on a human or a situation:
- このデータは〜を示している → acceptable in academic Japanese
- "The news made her happy" → その知らせを聞いて彼女は喜んだ (not その知らせは彼女を幸せにした)

## 3. Passive overuse

English passive → Japanese active with omitted agent is often better: "It is believed that…" → 〜と考えられている is fine; "The meeting was attended by 20 people" → 会議には20人が出席した.

## 4. Nominalization chains

"The implementation of the improvement of the system" → システムを改善するための実装… → better: システムの改善を実施する. Avoid の連続 (の の の): 東京の大学の学生の意見の… → restructure.

## 5. 〜することができる / 〜を行う / 〜となっている

- 〜することができる → 〜できる
- 調査を行う → 調査する (unless formal register needs 実施する)
- 〜となっている → 〜だ / 〜である when no change of state is meant

## 6. Relative clauses and sentence length

Long English sentences with which/that clauses → split into two or three Japanese sentences. A single sentence over ~80 characters should be checked.

## 7. Connectives

- "However" at every paragraph → しかし / ただし / 一方 / もっとも, chosen by logic; or no connective.
- "And" → don't translate mechanically as そして.
- "Also" → また (not overused), さらに.

## 8. 文体 consistency

Never mix です・ます and だ・である in body text. Headings, bullets, captions, and table cells may use 体言止め. Quoted speech follows the speaker.

## 9. Over-literal idioms

"It's not rocket science" → それほど難しいことではない; "a piece of cake" → 朝飯前 / 簡単. Translate the meaning; use a Japanese idiom only if the register allows.

## 10. Plural markers

Don't add たち/ら unless needed: "students" → 学生 (学生たち only when referring to a specific group).

## 11. "Please" and imperatives

"Please note that…" → なお、〜 / 〜にご注意ください. Not 〜であることを注意してください.

## 12. Katakana overuse

Prefer an established Japanese word when it exists and the register is not technical: "approach" → 方法 / 手法 (アプローチ is fine in business and academic Japanese, but don't stack loanwords: アプローチのフレームワークのスキーム ✗).

## 13. Titles and names

- Foreign names in katakana: use established spellings (ジョン・スミス; middle dot ・ between given and family name). If no established spelling, transliterate and keep the original in parentheses on first use: ベネディクト・アンダーソン（Benedict Anderson）.
- Book titles → 『』; article/chapter titles → 「」. Use the published Japanese title if one exists; otherwise translate and add the original.

## 14. Numbers

30,000 → 3万 (in prose) or 30,000 (in tables/technical); 100 million → 1億. "Fiscal year 2026" → 2026年度.
